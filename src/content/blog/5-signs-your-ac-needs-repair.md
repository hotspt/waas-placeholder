Summer is coming, and the last thing you want is your air conditioner quitting on the hottest day of the year. Most AC failures don't happen out of nowhere — they give you warning signs weeks in advance. Here are the five most common ones we see on service calls.

## 1. Weak airflow from the vents

If you're holding your hand up to a vent and barely feeling anything, you have a problem. Common causes range from a clogged filter (easy fix) to a failing blower motor (not easy). Start by replacing your filter. If airflow is still weak after that, call us.

## 2. Warm air instead of cool

If the system is running but the air coming out isn't cold, you're likely low on refrigerant or have a failing compressor. Neither is a DIY fix. Refrigerant is regulated and requires a licensed technician — and the leak that caused the low charge needs to be found and repaired, not just topped off.

## 3. Strange noises

Grinding, squealing, banging, or rattling are all signs that something mechanical is wrong. A grinding sound often means a failing motor bearing. A squealing sound could be a worn belt. Banging could be a loose or broken internal component. Any of these can turn into a much bigger repair if ignored.

## 4. Musty or unusual smells

A musty smell usually means mold or mildew in the system — often in the ductwork or on the evaporator coil. That's a health issue as much as an HVAC one. A burning smell is worse: shut the system off immediately and call us.

## 5. Rising energy bills

If your energy bill is climbing but your usage habits haven't changed, your AC is working harder than it should. That's a symptom of a system that's losing efficiency — dirty coils, low refrigerant, aging compressor, or duct leaks.

## What to do if you notice any of these

Don't wait. Small AC problems become big AC problems quickly, and they always seem to fail on the worst possible day. Call us, and we'll come out, diagnose the issue, and give you a straight answer about what it needs — repair or replacement, and what it'll cost either way.
